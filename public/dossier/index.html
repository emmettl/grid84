Grid/84 Background documents

The working papers

Grid/84 is a playback engine for the history and doctrine of strategic nuclear weapons, and it publishes what it stands on. These are the research dossiers behind the models: the sources read, the figures found, the disputes left open, and the numbers in wide circulation that do not survive checking.

Last revised 10 September 2026 Return to the atlas

Why these exist

A model of a nuclear exchange is a chain of numbers, and a chain is worth the weakest link in it. Every figure the engine uses carries a source and an evidence tier, but a tier in a tooltip cannot show how the figure was arrived at, which papers disagree about it, or what had to be assumed when nothing was published at all. These documents do that work at length.

They follow one convention throughout: a quoted figure is read from the source, not from a summary of it; a composite is labelled as a composite; and where a widely repeated number cannot be traced to a paper, that is stated rather than quietly dropped.

One of them is a different kind of thing and is marked as such. An explainer is not about the literature; it is about the subject, written for a reader coming to it for the first time, and it is the one to start with.

    The Soot Question

    What is established about the climatic effects of nuclear war, what rests on a single modelling group, and where the real disagreement lies — which is not where it is usually said to be. Covers the source term, the Reisner dispute, the climate response by injection mass, the Xia famine figures and their four dropped conditions, and ozone.

    Hitting a Bullet with a Bullet

    The published record on ballistic and hypersonic missile defence, figure by figure. Which interceptor speeds are official — almost none of them are — how the test records are counted and by whom, what the Gulf War Patriot figures actually were and how they were revised, what the Ukrainian dataset shows, and where the phrase comes from. Eleven systems across four phases of flight.

  • Paper 03 · Weapons effects

    Sixty Feet at One Kilotonne

    Every prompt-effect formula in the engine traced to its section of Glasstone and Dolan's The Effects of Nuclear Weapons, with the computed values set beside the handbook's own worked examples. Blast, thermal, initial radiation, the fireball, the crater and the fallout pattern — including the places where the book declines to give a number, and two results that fall out of putting its sections beside each other.

  • Paper 04 · Targeting

    The Third Front

    From 1964 China moved its war industry into the mountains of the interior, explicitly because of the atomic bomb, at two fifths of national construction investment for sixteen years. It is the largest thing any state has done in response to nuclear targeting — and it is the reason a target list drawn from population centres misreads the country, by between seventy and two hundred and fifty kilometres.

    Naughton, Meyskens, Lewis & Xue, Zhang · 1964–1980Where this engine’s own method fails
