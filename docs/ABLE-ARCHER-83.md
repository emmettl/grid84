# ABLE ARCHER//83

[SIOP//62 brief](SIOP-62.md) · [Roadmap](../ROADMAP.md) · [Validation](VALIDATION.md)

**The second execution study. A counterfactual on a documented exercise: what if the Soviet forces that were readying to preempt a NATO strike under cover of Able Archer had been ordered to.**

**Status:** built, 9 September 2026, at `#/study/able-archer-83`; see the section at the end.

## Thesis

SIOP//62 enacts a plan that was written and never executed. Able Archer 83 is the opposite object: an exercise that was executed and never became a plan, during which the other side prepared for the real thing. The record is unusually good on both sides and unusually candid about its own uncertainty, which makes it the right second study for an engine whose visual language is built on evidence tiers.

The exercise ran from 7 to 11 November 1983 in a scenario in which Warsaw Pact forces had opened hostilities across Allied Command Europe on 4 November and used chemical weapons by the 6th. NATO simulated escalation to DEFCON 1, requested nuclear release on the evening of 8 November, employed weapons on the morning of the 9th, and executed a follow-on nuclear strike on the morning of the 11th. It did so with new features: a 170-flight radio-silent airlift of 19,000 soldiers, a move to the alternate war headquarters, new release procedures with consultations in Washington and London, and "slips of the tongue" calling B-52 sorties "strikes". On the other side, Soviet air units in East Germany and Poland went to heightened readiness, fighter-bombers of the Group of Soviet Forces in Germany to thirty-minute alert, and the Soviet 4th Air Army began "preparations for immediate use of nuclear weapons". The President's Foreign Intelligence Advisory Board concluded in 1990 that "at least some Soviet forces were preparing to preempt or counterattack a NATO strike launched under cover of Able Archer", that the United States "may have inadvertently placed our relations with the Soviet Union on a hair trigger", and that the one American general who declined to answer alert with alert "acted correctly out of instinct, not informed guidance".

The study enacts the branch that instinct avoided: Soviet frontal aviation and theatre missiles strike first, on the last morning of the exercise, against NATO's nuclear delivery means and the bases they fly from, and NATO's surviving theatre forces answer. It is a European war of hours, not the SIOP's fifteen, and its population grid is 1983.

## The constraint and the tiers

Everything on the readout carries a tier, as in SIOP//62, and the withheld tier will be busier here than there.

| Tier | What falls in it |
| --- | --- |
| **Documented** | The exercise timeline and scenario; the Soviet alert measures the intelligence record reports; RYaN's existence, start and indicator lists; the 1984 estimates and the 1990 board's judgements; the dates of the Pershing II and cruise-missile arrivals that framed the crisis |
| **Reconstructed** | Orders of battle for both sides in November 1983 from published force tables; the exercise's simulated release translated into targets and timings |
| **Inferred** | Every Soviet target, the timing of a preemptive order, the weapon loads on the 4th Air Army's aircraft, NATO's response under attack |

## What the study enacts

The branch, in the register of the evidence tiers:

1. **The exercise as documented**, played on the clock from 4 November: the scenario's fictional war as a ghost layer, the real airlift and headquarters move as documented movement, the release simulation on the 8th, 9th, 10th and 11th as documented events with no geometry, because nothing left the ground.
2. **The Soviet readiness as documented**: fighter-bomber regiments in East Germany and Poland at thirty-minute alert, the 4th Air Army loading, drawn at reconstructed bases with the units the record names and the units it does not badged accordingly.
3. **The inferred order**: on the morning of 11 November, when the exercise executes its follow-on strike, the Soviet command reads it as real. Frontal aviation and the theatre missiles of the Group of Soviet Forces in Germany strike NATO's nuclear delivery means: the quick-reaction-alert airfields, the Pershing and cruise-missile sites then arriving in Britain and West Germany, the nuclear artillery and Lance units, and the headquarters. The targeting is inferred; the readout says so on every mark.
4. **NATO's answer** from what survives, under the release procedures the exercise had just rehearsed, against the Soviet forward airfields and missile brigades. Also inferred.
5. **The outcome** over HYDE 1983 for West Germany, East Germany, Poland, the Low Countries and Britain, by the same two-number method, with the same blast-only and fire bounds, and the plume lab under November winds for a theatre whose fallout falls on the people fighting in it.

The finding the study is built to reach is the PFIAB's own: the difference between the branch and the record is one order, and one general's refusal to give the matching one.

## Built, 9 September 2026

`#/study/able-archer-83` plays the week and the branch on one clock of hours from the morning of 11 November. The order of battle is built by [`scripts/build-order-of-battle-1983.py`](../scripts/build-order-of-battle-1983.py): fifteen NATO strike wings and squadrons, the three Pershing 1a battalions, Greenham Common drawn withheld with its sixteen missiles three days away, eight headquarters and ports; on the other side nine Soviet regiments in East Germany and Poland, four missile brigades, five SS-20 garrisons standing for the western share of 405 launchers, and two headquarters. The exercise's ladder is [`data/able-archer/exercise-1983.json`](../data/able-archer/exercise-1983.json), ten steps from the scenario's fictional 4 November to the record's verdicts, each with a source; a dozen of the 172 radio-silent airlift missions are drawn from the east-coast ports to Rhein-Main.

**The finding.** The first twenty minutes decide the answer. The Scud brigades fire at H+15 and land four minutes later; the SS-20s fire at H+10 and land at H+20; NATO's fifteen-minute alert forces leave at H+20 on warning of the first launches. Every Pershing garrison and most airfields are struck before that, so the answer is a dozen weapons from the two or three bases the first wave did not reach, and the generated force, four hours later, never leaves. The Warsaw Pact's own strike is small by the list, not the force: 71 weapons cover NATO's nuclear delivery means and headquarters three deep, and 868 stay unassigned because the rule gives them no target. The Soviet regiments and garrisons are reconstructed from the divisions the unit histories name, and the readout says so on each.

## Open questions

- Which units, exactly, did the intelligence record see readying? The public documents name the 4th Air Army and GSFG fighter-bombers; the regiment-level picture is in the still-redacted material and in Soviet sources not yet read.
- What did the Soviet General Staff's own plan for a preemptive theatre strike look like in 1983? The study can use published Warsaw Pact exercise material (the Seven Days to the River Rhine plan of 1979 is the nearest published analogue) as a reconstructed template, labelled as such.
- How far should the study follow escalation past the theatre? The brief stops at the theatre exchange. Anything beyond it is the SIOP's successor, SIOP-6, and nothing of it is public.
- Register. This is the study in which the people preparing to strike were afraid, and the readout should carry that from RYaN's indicator lists onward: churches, banks and blood banks watched for a war that was not coming.
